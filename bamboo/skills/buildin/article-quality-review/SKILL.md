# Article Quality Review

## When to Use

Use this skill after a draft has been written and edited for tone, but before packaging or publishing. It is especially useful for WeChat public-account articles, technical explainers, research summaries, platform reposts, and content derived from extracted materials.

This skill checks whether the article is worth reading, not merely whether it sounds natural.

## Review Standard

A publishable article should have:

- a clear reader promise: the reader can tell what they will understand or gain;
- a coherent main line: each section advances the article, not just summarizes source fragments;
- high information density: few sentences exist only as transitions, hype, or repetition;
- grounded claims: important conclusions are supported by source material, data, figures, examples, or verified background;
- useful explanation: difficult ideas are explained at the level the target reader needs;
- meaningful visuals: each image/table is introduced and interpreted, not dropped in as decoration;
- honest boundaries: uncertainty, limitations, missing context, or unverified links are visible;
- a concrete ending: the final section leaves useful takeaways, not a generic wrap-up.

## Waste To Remove

- Opening paragraphs that only say the topic is important.
- Section intros that repeat the heading.
- Generic transitions: "Next, let's look at...", "It is worth noting that..." when no real contrast follows.
- Repeated claims in different wording.
- Lists where only one or two items matter.
- Figure captions that restate the filename or title instead of explaining what the reader should see.
- Unsupported hype, especially "breakthrough", "revolutionary", "first truly", "completely changes", unless the evidence really justifies it.
- Long background that does not unlock the current article.
- Conclusions that summarize every section but add no judgment.

## Workflow

1. Read the draft and the available source notes/assets.
2. Identify the article's intended reader and the one-sentence central value.
3. Make an internal outline from the draft. If a section does not serve the central value, mark it for cutting or rewriting.
4. Check factual grounding:
   - claims with numbers;
   - comparisons and "better/faster/first" claims;
   - publication metadata, dates, links, authors, institutions;
   - conclusions drawn from figures or tables.
5. Check explanatory quality:
   - prerequisites introduced before they are needed;
   - technical terms explained once, not repeatedly;
   - examples used where abstraction is hard;
   - figures/tables interpreted in prose.
6. Remove or rewrite low-value sentences. Prefer cutting over polishing filler.
7. Strengthen weak sections by adding source-grounded specifics, not generic commentary.
8. If missing external information blocks a good article, stop and request/search for it before finalizing.
9. Return either:
   - the revised draft in embedded mode; or
   - a concise review with must-fix items, should-fix items, and suggested edits when asked for review only.

## Pass Criteria

Before the article can be packaged:

- The opening states a concrete problem, result, or reader payoff within the first three paragraphs.
- Every section earns its place.
- Every important figure/table is explained near where it appears.
- No major claim depends on an unchecked source when checking was possible.
- The article has fewer words after the review unless new source-grounded information was added.
- The final takeaway is specific enough that it could not fit any similar article.
